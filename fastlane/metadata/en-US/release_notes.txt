Surf ratings get stricter — and truer.

The engine could score a storm 75/100. Three guards now apply:
- Too much wind closes the session. The threshold depends on direction: offshore holds a wave up, onshore tears it apart. The same speed no longer counts the same regardless of where it comes from.
- A flat sea scores nothing. Below 30 cm of swell there is nothing to surf.
- Period is now PEAK period, the one surfers actually talk about, whenever that data exists. A long period means swell that has travelled; a short one means chop raised by local wind.

Spots that showed four stars will show two, and some GO windows will disappear. That is the point: a rating that says yes to everything is worthless.

The wind ACTUALLY MEASURED is drawn on the chart, beneath the forecast: speed and gust, exactly as the anemometer recorded them. The gap between what was announced and what blew reads at a glance — no need to take our word for it.

That trace is now rebuilt from the history the stations publish themselves, in 30-minute steps: no more islands of isolated points. Where measurement is missing, the line breaks. A silence of several hours will never be filled with an invented line.

Honesty:
- Three indices (fishing, swimming, launching) announced "glassy, ideal" where no wave measurement existed at all. With no data the factor now disappears instead of inventing calm.

Spots:
- Andernos-les-Bains (Le Betey), Hourtin-Plage and Lacanau Ocean join the catalogue.
- "Lacanau" becomes "Lacanau (offshore)": that tide reference point sits 29 km out to sea. Its tides are right; its wind is not the wind on the beach.
- A spot you pinned yourself now takes the general weather of the nearest verified location, within 8 km. A pin dropped a few kilometres offshore was reading open-water wind, and could rank a sheltered bay ahead of an ocean beach.

The weather strip now uses the colour spectrum of the wind ribbon above it, at full intensity.

The app uses less network and less battery on every refresh.

Fixes:
- The live wind label no longer hides the curves.
- When the forecast service hits its daily limit, the app says so — instead of sending you off to check your connection.

Fair winds and good waves!
